How we work

Louisville Pro Plumbing serves Louisville, CO and the towns next door. We take calls at any hour and give a clear price before work starts.

We tell you what we find, including when a repair is not worth it. A new heater costs more than a repair, and we explain why we recommend it.

Verify any plumber

You should be able to check anyone you let into your home. The Colorado Division of Professions and Occupations runs a license lookup at https://apps2.colorado.gov/dora/licensing/Lookup/LicenseLookup.aspx. Use it for us or for anyone else.

For permits, the City of Louisville's Building Safety division answers questions at (303) 335-4584.

How a call goes

A person answers the phone. We ask where you are, what you see and what you have done so far. If water is spreading, we tell you how to close the main valve. If a drain is backing up, we tell you to stop using water.

Then we tell you whether we can come today and what the visit involves. Nothing about the call is a form, a chat bot or a callback queue.

What a clear price means

A clear price means you hear the number before the work starts, after the plumber has seen the problem. If the scope changes, we say so before we continue.

We do not promise an arrival time. We promise to explain what we see and to tell you what each option costs.

Louisville is not a generic market

According to the City of Louisville's 2026 water quality report, the supply comes mostly from South Boulder Creek and chlorine is the disinfectant. The Colorado Geological Survey calls expansive soil Colorado's most significant geologic hazard. The sewer service line is the owner's responsibility under the City's detail sheet.

Those facts shape how we diagnose a problem, and we put them on the pages where they matter.

What we do

We handle drain cleaning, water heater repair, leak detection, sewer line repair and the rest of the work on our services page.

We serve Lafayette, Superior and Broomfield as well as Louisville.

When we say no

Sometimes the honest answer is that a job is not ours. A leak on the street side of the curb stop belongs to the City. A cracked foundation belongs to a specialist. A tank that is leaking from the steel needs replacing, not repairing.

We would rather tell you that on the phone than take a visit that does not solve your problem.

Working with insurers, landlords and builders

Many calls involve someone else: an insurance adjuster, a landlord or a general contractor. We write down what we found and what we did in plain language, so you can hand it over without translating.

If you are a landlord, tell us the unit and the tenant contact when you call. If you are rebuilding, we coordinate the rough-in with your builder so inspections do not stall.

Residential and commercial work

Homeowners call for drains, heaters and leaks. Landlords and property managers call for several units at once, and businesses call when a restaurant sink or an office restroom stops working. We handle all three, and our commercial plumbing page covers what changes for a business.

Planned work, such as repiping, installations and water heater installs, is scheduled around your project.
