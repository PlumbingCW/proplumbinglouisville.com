Find the source before the fix

Basement waterproofing is the work of keeping groundwater and surface water out of a basement. A wet basement can have several causes, and each needs a different answer.

Our approach starts with the plumbing. We check for supply leaks, a failed water heater, a running sump pump and a sewer backup before any drainage work is proposed. Call to ask about timing.

Rule out a leak with leak detection, and look at the pump with sump pump service. We handle basement calls in Lafayette homes as well.

Common sources of basement water

Plumbing leak

A weeping fitting or a failed supply line can mimic groundwater. A meter test settles it.

Surface runoff

Gutters, downspouts and a lot that slopes toward the house send water to the foundation.

Rising groundwater

A high water table after snowmelt or heavy rain pushes water through the slab or walls.

Window wells

A well without a drain fills in a storm and spills through the window.

A quick home check before you call anyone

Walk the outside of the house after rain. Look for gutters that overflow, downspouts that dump at the foundation and soil that slopes toward the wall. Fixing these costs far less than interior work.

Inside, note where and when water shows up. Water at one corner after storms points to surface runoff. Water along the floor in dry weather points to a leak or rising groundwater.

Finished basements and moisture

Moisture in a finished basement may show up as a smell, a stain or flooring that lifts before you ever see water. Early signs deserve attention because mold grows where materials stay damp.

A dehumidifier helps with humidity but does not solve a water source. Find and fix the source first.

Soil and slabs along the Front Range

The Colorado Geological Survey calls expansive soil Colorado's most significant geologic hazard. Swelling soil can crack basement floors and heave slabs, which opens paths for water. The Survey lists cracked floors as one of the signs.

We do not diagnose your soil. We recommend a soil or foundation specialist if cracks are wide or growing, and we focus on the plumbing and drainage side. Surface grading and gutter extensions often cost far less than interior work.

Gravity underdrains in some subdivisions

The City of Louisville's construction detail sheet mentions gravity underdrains in certain subdivisions and says they are the property owner's responsibility. If your home has one, a camera can tell whether it is clear or crushed.

We do not assume your home has one. If you suspect it, ask the City or check your plans, and we can inspect the outlet.
