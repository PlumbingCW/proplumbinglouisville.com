All Drains in the House Clogged: A Louisville, CO Guide

One slow drain is a nuisance. All of them at once is a main line problem. Here is how to confirm it, what to do while you wait and why it matters who owns the pipe.

If sewage is backing up, do this

Every gallon you add can end up on the floor.

  • Stop running water: no laundry, dishes, showers or flushing.
  • Keep people and pets away from backed-up water, which can carry bacteria.
  • If the lowest drain is overflowing, move belongings out of its path.
  • Do not pour drain cleaner into a backed-up line.
  • Call us and tell us which fixtures are affected.

Why every drain slows at once

Every drain in a house joins a main line that runs out to the sewer. A blockage in the main holds back the flow from all of them. The lowest fixtures, such as a basement shower or floor drain, show it first.

Common causes are tree roots, grease, flushed wipes, a sagging section of pipe and a broken or offset joint.

Signs it is the main line

Several fixtures slow together

Sinks, tubs and toilets all drain slowly at the same time.

Gurgling in unrelated fixtures

Running the washer makes a toilet bubble.

Water coming up a low drain

The basement floor drain backs up when you run water upstairs.

A smell near drains

A sewer odor in the basement.

What clearing the main involves

We cable the line from a cleanout and run the camera to look at the cause. If grease or roots are the problem, jetting may be the better tool. If the pipe has a defect, cleaning is only a short-term fix.

Our sewer line cleaning page explains the process, and hydro jetting covers when a water jet is the better choice.

Preventing a repeat

Keep grease out of the drain. Flush only toilet paper. If roots are the problem, a camera inspection can set a cleaning schedule or show that repair is wiser.

A drain that clogs again within months is telling you something. We would rather show you the footage than clear it a third time.

Find your cleanout

A cleanout is a capped pipe that gives access to the main line. It is often in the basement, the crawlspace, the garage or outside near the house. Knowing where it is saves time during a backup.

Do not open it when the line is full of water, because the pressure can send waste over the floor. Let the plumber do it.

What causes repeat main line clogs

Roots, a sag in the pipe and a joint that has shifted are the common causes. Each catches debris. Cleaning removes the symptom, and a camera finds the cause.

A line that clogs again within a year deserves a camera visit. See sewer inspection for what the footage shows.

Rentals and landlords

Tenants should tell the landlord at once about a backup. The owner is responsible for the line, and the sooner it is cleared, the less damage.

Landlords can call us with the address and tenant contact, and we send a written summary.

What not to do

Do not keep flushing to see if it clears. Each flush adds a gallon or more with nowhere to go. Do not run the washer, and do not pour chemical opener into a line that is already full.

Do not hand-rake a cleanout while water stands in it. Wait for the plumber, who has the gear to handle it safely.

The sewer line is the owner's responsibility in Louisville

The City of Louisville's construction detail sheet says the sewer service line is the property owner's responsibility, including closed-circuit TV inspection and repair. If the blockage is in the City's main, the City handles it.

A camera shows which side of the connection holds the blockage, which is why we run one when a main line clogs. That avoids paying for work on the wrong pipe.
