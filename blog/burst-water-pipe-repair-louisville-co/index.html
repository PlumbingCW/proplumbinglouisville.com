A burst pipe is a race between you and the water. This guide covers what to do in the first minutes, what a repair looks like and how to protect an insurance claim.

The first ten minutes

These steps limit the damage before a plumber arrives.

  • Shut off the main water valve and open a low tap to drain the lines.
  • Switch off power to affected rooms if you can do it safely, and never wade through water to reach a panel.
  • Move belongings, rugs and electronics out of the water.
  • Photograph and video the damage before cleanup.
  • Call a plumber and then your insurer.

Why pipes burst

Freezing is the best-known cause. Water expands as it turns to ice and pressure builds between the ice and a closed faucet. The pipe usually splits where the ice has formed, and it may leak only when it thaws.

Corrosion, high water pressure and ground movement are the others. A pipe that gives way in summer often had an old weak spot or a fitting that failed under pressure.

Where we find bursts in Louisville homes

Exterior walls

Cold air reaches the pipe, especially where insulation is thin.

Crawlspaces and garages

Unheated areas with exposed lines.

Hose bibs

A hose left attached lets water freeze inside the spigot and the pipe behind it.

Buried service lines

Ground movement or corrosion can fail the line between the street and the house.

What the repair involves

The plumber cuts out the failed section, installs new pipe and fittings and tests under pressure. If the pipe is in a finished wall, a small opening is made and then patched or covered.

If the same line has several weak points, a longer replacement is wiser than patching. Our burst pipe repair page explains how we decide.

Drying and insurance

Wet drywall and insulation hold moisture. A water mitigation company can dry the house and check for hidden moisture. Many homeowner policies cover sudden burst-pipe damage, though policies vary.

Keep photos, receipts and the plumber's written report. If the break is on the buried line, read about the owner's portion on our water service line repair page.

Prevention before the next cold snap

Disconnect hoses from outdoor spigots before the first hard freeze. Insulate pipes in the garage, the crawlspace and on exterior walls. Seal air leaks near pipes with caulk or foam.

If you will be away, leave the heat on at a moderate setting. A frozen pipe often bursts while the house is empty, and nobody is there to shut the water off.

What to tell the plumber

Tell us where the water is coming from, whether you closed the main and how long it ran. Tell us whether the pipe was frozen, and whether this is a new problem or a repeat.

Describe the pipe material if you know it, such as copper, plastic or gray steel. It helps us bring the right fittings.

Questions your insurer may ask

Expect questions about when you found the leak, how long water ran and what you did to stop it. Answer plainly and keep the plumber's report.

Do not discard damaged items until the adjuster has seen them or you have photographed them. Replacement decisions are easier with a record.

Burst pipes in rentals and shared buildings

Tenants should shut the unit's valve if there is one, then call the landlord and the plumber. Report the time you noticed the leak in writing, since responsibility and insurance often depend on it.

In shared buildings, a burst can reach neighbors below. Tell the manager at once so other units can be checked for water.

Who owns which pipe in Louisville

The City of Louisville's construction detail sheet splits the water service into a property-owner portion and a city-owned portion, with a curb stop between them. A burst on the street side of the curb stop is the City's to repair, and one on the house side is the owner's.
