When the shower will not stay on and the spout keeps spilling, the diverter is not doing its job. Some diverters are a quick fix and some need a plumber to open the wall.

What a diverter does

A diverter sends water to the tub spout or to the showerhead. On many tubs it is a pull-up knob on top of the spout. On others it is a handle on the wall with three positions, or a separate valve.

When it fails, water dribbles from the spout while the shower is running, or the shower fades as soon as you let go. A failed diverter wastes hot water and pressure.

Find your diverter type

Pull-up knob on the spout

A rubber gasket inside the spout seals when you pull the knob. It wears and leaks, and it is the simplest fix.

Slip-on or threaded spout

The diverter is built into the spout. Replacing the spout often solves it.

Wall-mounted diverter valve

A three-valve setup. The diverter handle sits between the hot and cold handles.

Single-handle with push-pull

A cartridge controls both flow and diversion. A new cartridge may be needed.

What to do: replacing a spout diverter

Replace the spout when the diverter inside is worn.

1

Check how it attaches

Look under the spout for a hex screw, or try turning it. A threaded spout unscrews by hand.

2

Remove the spout

Loosen the screw or turn the spout counterclockwise. Wrap a cloth around it to protect the finish.

3

Measure and match

Check the pipe length and the type. Take the old spout to the store.

4

Install the new spout

Wrap the pipe threads with tape, thread the new spout on and seal with a thin bead of caulk.

5

Test

Run the tub and the shower, and check for leaks at the wall.

When the wall has to be opened

If the diverter is a wall valve or a cartridge inside the wall and the trim comes off, we may need access from behind or through the tile. A leaking valve inside the wall can rot framing, so do not ignore it.

Our faucet repair page covers tub and shower valves. If you are remodeling the bath, see plumbing installation for options.

A small drip is still a leak

Water that sneaks past a failed diverter while you shower wastes hot water. Over a month it adds up on both the water bill and the water heater.

If you see staining on a ceiling below the tub, or the wall near the tub is soft, call. That is a leak inside the wall.

Tools and supplies for a spout swap

You need a hex key set or a large wrench, plumber's tape, a utility knife and a small tube of silicone caulk. A cloth keeps the finish from scratching. Keep the old spout so you can match the length.

Measure from the wall to the end of the pipe stub if the spout slips on. A copper stub sets the spout length, and a spout that is too long will not seat.

Single-handle tubs and pressure balance

A single-handle valve with a diverter often has a cartridge that does both jobs. A worn cartridge can cause a drip and a loss of temperature control. The part number is stamped on the old cartridge.

If the shower scalds when a toilet flushes, the cartridge may be the problem. That is a safety issue, and a plumber should look at it.

Caulk and water in the wall

Gaps around the spout and the tile let shower water run into the wall. Re-caulking with silicone each year is cheap, and it prevents rot behind the tub.

If caulk keeps failing, the tile may be flexing or the spout may be loose. Both are worth a look before a small leak becomes a rebuild.
