How to Repair a Toilet Flush Valve in Louisville, CO

A toilet that runs, hisses or flushes weakly usually has a worn flush valve, flapper or fill valve. Many are a one-hour, low-cost fix. Here is how to tell which part has failed.

What the flush valve does

The flush valve is the large opening at the bottom of the tank. A rubber flapper or seal covers it. When you press the handle, the flapper lifts, water rushes into the bowl and the flapper drops back to seal the tank.

If the seal is worn, warped or covered in mineral film, water slips past it and into the bowl all day. The fill valve then tops the tank up again and again, which is why the toilet seems to run or cycle by itself.

Match the symptom to the part

Toilet runs on and off

A leaking flapper or flush valve seat. Add food coloring to the tank. If color shows in the bowl without a flush, the seal is leaking.

Hissing after a flush

A worn fill valve that will not shut off cleanly, or a float set too high.

Weak flush

A chain that is too tight, a low water level or a partly blocked rim.

Handle sticks or jiggles

A loose handle arm or a chain tangled under the flapper.

What to do: a basic flapper replacement

Plan on under an hour. Buy a flapper that matches the brand and the valve size, which is printed inside the tank or on the old flapper.

1

Shut the water

Close the supply valve behind the toilet and flush to empty the tank.

2

Remove the old flapper

Unclip it from the ears at the sides of the overflow tube and unhook the chain.

3

Clean the seat

Wipe the ring the flapper sits on with a cloth. Mineral film here causes many leaks.

4

Fit the new flapper

Clip it on, hook the chain with a little slack and open the valve.

5

Test

Flush twice and repeat the food coloring check after twenty minutes.

When the whole flush valve needs replacing

If the seat is pitted or cracked, a new flapper will not seal. Then the whole flush valve, which sits through the tank floor, has to be replaced. That means removing the tank or its bolts, so it is more work than a flapper.

An old toilet with a cracked tank or a rocking base is a better candidate for replacement. Our toilet repair page explains how we decide between repairing and replacing.

A running toilet is a hidden water bill

A small leak at the flapper can pass many gallons a day without any sound. If your water bill has climbed, check the toilets first, then try the meter test on our leak detection page.

If there are signs of water at the base of the toilet or on the ceiling below, stop and call. A seal failure there is a different problem from a flush valve.

Parts to buy and what to skip

Bring the old flapper or take a photo of the tank label. Flappers come in two inch and three inch sizes, and a mismatch will never seal. A universal kit is fine when the brand is unknown, though a brand part often fits better.

Skip the drop-in tank tablets. Bleach and some cleaners soften rubber and can shorten the life of the very part you just replaced.

Rubber and plastic parts in a toilet tank last different lengths of time depending on the water, so ask the City or test your tap if parts keep failing.

Many homeowners here replace a flapper every few years as a matter of course. If yours is sticky or gummy to the touch, it is worn out. Parts from a home center are cheap, and the fix is within reach of most people.
