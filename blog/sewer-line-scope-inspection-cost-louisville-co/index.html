What a sewer scope is

A sewer scope is an inspection by a camera on a flexible cable. The plumber feeds it into the line through a cleanout and watches the picture as it travels toward the street.

It shows roots, cracks, offsets, sags, grease and collapsed sections, and it shows the pipe material. A recording and a location note make the report useful.

What moves the price of a sewer scope

We do not publish a flat price, because the factors vary house to house. These are the ones that matter.

AccessA working cleanout makes the job quick. Without one, the camera may have to enter through a toilet.
Length of lineA longer run takes more time.
Cleaning firstA line with heavy buildup may need cabling or jetting before the camera passes.
LocatingMarking a defect's position above ground is an added step.
Report and recordingA written report with video is worth asking for.

The pipe is the one big system an ordinary inspection cannot see.

If the same drain keeps clogging, the camera shows why.

Older house or mature trees

Roots and older pipe materials are risk factors.

After a backup

To see whether the line is damaged or just blocked.

Reading the result

The best reports tell you the pipe material, the footage where each defect sits and what the defect is. A root intrusion at 30 feet is a different job from a collapse at 60 feet.

If the camera finds a problem, we explain whether cleaning is enough, whether spot repair makes sense or whether replacement is the honest answer. See sewer line repair for the options.

Buying a home: use the scope in the deal

A scope done during the inspection period gives you leverage. A clean line is reassuring. A defect lets you ask for a repair, a credit or a lower price before you are locked in.

Ask for the video and the report, not just a thumbs up. If the seller will not allow a scope, that is information too.

What a scope cannot tell you

A camera sees the inside of the pipe. It does not measure soil pressure or the condition of every joint beyond the lens. A line that looks fine today can develop a problem later, and a scope is a snapshot.

It also cannot see past a full blockage. We clear the line first if we have to, which is why cleaning is a separate cost line.

Scope or full replacement planning

If the scope finds a defect, you have choices. A short defect may be a spot repair. A line with several defects may call for replacement, sometimes with a trenchless method.

Our sewer inspection page describes what we record, and the footage helps you compare any bids. Get a second opinion on a big replacement.

Questions to ask the company doing the scope

Ask whether the report includes video, a written summary and the footage where each defect sits. Ask whether they will mark the spot above ground.

Ask also whether they clean the line first when needed. A scope through a partly blocked pipe may miss defects. A clear, honest answer is a good sign.

Scoping a line before a remodel or landscaping project

If you plan a new patio, a large tree or an addition near the sewer path, a scope first shows where the pipe runs and what shape it is in. Building over a weak line is an expensive surprise.

Ask the camera operator to mark the line's path and depth above ground. Your builder will want that information.

Why the line is your responsibility in Louisville

The City of Louisville's construction detail sheet makes the sewer service line the property owner's responsibility, including closed-circuit TV inspection and repair. In a purchase, that means a defect in the line is yours after closing.

The sheet also mentions gravity underdrains in certain subdivisions as an owner responsibility. If your home might have one, ask the seller and the City about it before you buy.
