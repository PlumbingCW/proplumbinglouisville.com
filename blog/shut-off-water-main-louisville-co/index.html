How to Shut Off the Water Main in a Louisville, CO Home

When a pipe bursts, the next minute matters more than the next call. Knowing where your shutoff is and how it works can save thousands of dollars of damage. Find it before you need it.

Three shutoffs you should know

Most homes have more than one shutoff. The first is at each fixture, such as the small valves under a sink or behind a toilet. The second is the main valve inside the house. The third is the curb stop outside, near the meter or the street.

For a burst, the main valve inside is the one you want. It stops water to the whole house. The curb stop is a back-up and is usually not for homeowners to operate.

Finding your main valve

Look in these places first.

1

Basement or utility room

Usually where the supply pipe comes through the foundation wall, close to the heater.

2

Crawlspace or garage

Look on the wall facing the street.

3

Near the meter

Some homes have a valve inside right after the meter.

4

Exterior box

A cover in the yard may hide the curb stop or the meter.

5

Label it

Put a tag on the valve so everyone in the house can find it.

Valve types

Gate valve

A round wheel handle. Turn it clockwise until it stops. Old gate valves can stick or break.

Ball valve

A lever handle. A quarter turn closes it, with the handle crosswise to the pipe.

Meter valve

A small valve right at the meter. Use it only if the inside valve fails.

Curb stop

A buried valve on the service line. Operating it needs a special key, and the City may require you not to.

Test it once a year

A valve that has not moved in years can seize. Close it and open it fully, then check for drips at the stem. If it sticks, or leaks, replace it before an emergency.

After a repair, open a tap at the highest point first to let air escape as the pipes refill. See burst pipe repair for what to do next.

Use the meter to check for hidden leaks

With every fixture off, a moving meter dial means water is escaping. Close the main valve and look again. If the dial still moves, the leak is before your valve.

That test is described on our leak detection page, along with what to do when the dial keeps turning.

Teach everyone in the house

Show every adult and older child where the valve is and which way it turns. Practice once. A tag on the valve with an arrow marked closed helps.

If you rent, ask the landlord where the main is. Tenants have flooded units because nobody knew.

If the valve will not turn

Do not force a stuck gate valve with a pipe wrench. It can snap the stem and make things worse. Try the meter valve or call the plumber and the City.

A valve that sticks is a valve that needs replacing, and a ball valve replacement is a short, planned job.

Winterizing a vacant house

Closing the main and draining the lines is common before leaving a house empty for a long winter. Open the lowest tap and the highest to drain the pipes, and drain the water heater if it will be off.

Keep in mind that a drained system with the heat off still needs protection from freezing at fixtures and traps. A plumber can winterize the whole house.

Who owns what in Louisville

The City of Louisville's construction detail sheet divides the water service into a property-owner portion and a city-owned portion, with a curb stop between them. Questions about the City side belong with the City.

The City's 2026 water quality report says the City is inventorying service lines for lead and gives 303-335-4741 for questions about your service line. If you find the line while you look for the shutoff, note the material.
