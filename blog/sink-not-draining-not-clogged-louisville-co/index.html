Sink Not Draining but Not Clogged in Louisville, CO

A sink that holds water with no visible clog is frustrating. The cause is often a vent, a partial blockage further down or a trap that is not where you think. Here is how to narrow it down.

Why a drain can be blocked without looking blocked

A drain needs three things to work: a clear pipe, a trap and a vent that lets air in behind the water. If any of them is partly blocked, water slows down even when the sink looks fine.

Grease that lines the pipe wall narrows the passage without closing it. Water still gets through, but slowly, and the sink drains with a gurgle.

Four causes to check, in order

Trap or pop-up stopper

Hair, soap scum and debris collect at the stopper and the P-trap. Remove and clean both.

Partial grease or soap buildup

A film narrows the pipe. Hot water helps a little and a cable clears it.

Blocked vent

A vent stack on the roof can clog with leaves or a bird nest. The drain gurgles and slows.

Main line partial blockage

If other drains are slow or a toilet gurgles when the sink drains, the problem is in the main.

A simple diagnosis you can do

1

Check the other fixtures

Run the tub, other sinks and flush a toilet. If more than one is slow, the problem is downstream.

2

Listen for gurgling

Gurgling in one fixture when another drains points to the vent or the main.

3

Clean the trap

Place a bucket, unscrew the trap and clear it. Reassemble and test.

4

Try a hand auger

Feed it into the drain past the trap and pull out the debris.

5

Stop there

If it is still slow, stop using chemical cleaners and call a plumber.

Why not to pour drain cleaner

Liquid drain openers can sit in a partly blocked pipe and damage older metal and plastic. If the clog does not clear, the water in the sink is now hazardous for the plumber who must open it.

A cable or a camera is a better next step. Our drain cleaning page describes how we clear lines, and sewer inspection covers what a camera shows.

When it is the main line

A basement floor drain that gurgles when a sink runs, or a toilet that bubbles, suggests the main line is partly blocked. Roots, grease and offset joints are common causes.

A camera tells us what and where. The City's construction detail sheet treats the sewer service line as the owner's responsibility, so findings matter.

Kitchen sink versus bathroom sink

A kitchen sink usually slows from grease and food film, and a disposal can mask it. A bathroom sink usually slows from hair and soap at the stopper and the trap.

That is why the first step differs. Clean the stopper in a bathroom sink. In a kitchen, run hot water with a little dish soap, then check whether the disposal side or the other side drains better.

Vents and why they matter

Every drain has a vent that lets air in behind the water. Without it, a drain slows and gurgles because the water cannot flow freely.

A vent blocked on the roof is not something to climb up and fix. A plumber can test it and clear it safely, and a camera can show whether the problem is the vent or the pipe below.

Signs you should stop and call

Call if the water backs up into a second fixture, if a sewer smell comes from a floor drain or if the sink drains fine and then stops again an hour later. Each of these points beyond the trap.

A plumber can usually tell which in a quick visit, and a camera settles it when the answer is not obvious.

Who is responsible for which pipe in Louisville

The City of Louisville's construction detail sheet says the sewer service line is the property owner's responsibility, including closed-circuit TV inspection and repair. The sheet also mentions gravity underdrains in certain subdivisions and treats them as the owner's responsibility.

If a clog is in the City's main in the street, the City handles it. A camera inspection shows which side of the connection the problem is on.
