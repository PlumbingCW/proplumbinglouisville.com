Venting a Tankless Water Heater in Louisville, CO Homes

A tankless gas heater burns more fuel at once than a tank, and it needs a vent built for that. Venting decides where the unit can go, how much the install costs and whether it is safe.

Why tankless venting is different

A tank heater is often vented through a large metal flue that rises on natural draft. Many tankless gas units use a sealed, power-vented system with plastic or stainless pipe, because the exhaust is cooler and has to be pushed out.

That is why a swap is not always a drop-in. The old flue may not suit the new unit, and the new vent may need a path through a wall or roof.

Vent types at a glance

TypeHow it worksTypical use
Direct ventDraws air from outside and exhausts outside through two pipes or one coaxial pipeClosets and living areas
Power ventA fan pushes exhaust outLonger runs
Natural draftHot gas rises up a metal flueOlder tank heaters
CondensingExtracts more heat and produces acidic condensateHigh-efficiency tankless, needs a drain

What to plan for

Vent path and length

Each manufacturer limits how long and how many elbows the vent may have.

Clearances

Terminations must stay clear of windows, doors and air intakes, as the manufacturer and the code specify.

Condensate drain

A condensing unit needs a drain line, and sometimes a neutralizer.

Combustion air

A tight house needs a way to bring air to the burner.

Louisville's elevation

Louisville sits at about 5,400 feet. Gas burners produce less heat at altitude, and manufacturers publish instructions for installing at high elevation. Some units adjust automatically and some need a setting changed.

We read the manual for your unit before we install it, and we do not rely on rules of thumb. If a contractor does not mention altitude, ask.

Choosing the location

A garage or an exterior wall often makes venting simple. A basement may need a longer run through a rim joist. An interior closet needs sealed combustion.

See our tankless water heater page for sizing, and water heater installation for permits and placement.

Common venting mistakes

Mixing vent pipe from different manufacturers is one. Each brand approves specific materials and fittings, and using another can void the warranty and create a hazard. Sloping the vent the wrong way is another, since condensate must drain away from the unit.

Terminating the vent too near a window or an air intake pulls exhaust back into the house. Distances come from the manufacturer's instructions and the code, so confirm them before drilling.

Gas line capacity

A tankless burner can draw several times the gas a tank burner does. The pipe from the meter to the heater has to carry that load. Old half-inch lines may need replacing, and the meter itself may need to be checked by the gas utility.

Ask the installer to size the gas line, not just the heater. A fine unit on an undersized line will shut down when two showers run.

Winter and freeze protection

Colorado winters bring hard freezes. A heater on an exterior wall or in an unheated garage needs freeze protection and a condensate line that will not ice up. The manufacturer's instructions say what is allowed.

If the heater is outdoors, check what the unit is rated for. A heater that is not rated for outdoors can fail the first winter.

What to hand your installer

Give the installer the model number, the planned location and any photos of the existing flue. Tell them about the elevation and the gas meter size if you know it.

A good installer will walk the vent path before quoting. A bid with no site visit is a guess.

Louisville permit and code for venting

The City of Louisville's Building Safety division lists replacing a water heater as needing a permit and answers questions at (303) 335-4584. The City adopted the 2024 International Plumbing Code, effective December 6, 2025, and the inspector confirms venting and gas piping.

We do not quote vent length or clearance numbers here. Those come from the manufacturer's instructions for your model and from the inspector, so confirm them for your project.
