A burst pipe is a race against the water

Burst pipe repair stops the flow, cuts out the failed section and replaces it with new pipe and fittings. It also includes drying advice, because water left in a wall causes more damage than the pipe itself.

Colorado winters make frozen pipes a real risk, especially in exterior walls, crawlspaces and unheated garages. The 24/7 emergency line answers at any hour.

If you cannot tell where the water starts, leak detection finds it. If the break is outside, water service line repair covers the buried pipe, and a pattern of failures may point to repiping. We answer burst calls in Superior too.

Pipe burst? Do these things in order

Minutes matter. This order limits the damage.

  • Shut the main water valve. It is often near the water heater, in the basement or where the line enters the house.
  • Turn off the electricity to any room with standing water if you can reach the breaker safely. Do not wade into water to reach a panel.
  • Open the lowest tap in the house to drain what is left in the lines.
  • Move furniture, rugs and electronics out of the water.
  • Photograph the damage, then call us.

After the repair: drying and documentation

Once the pipe is fixed, the water that spread still has to go. Wet drywall and insulation can hold moisture for days, and mold can start in that time. A water mitigation company can dry the structure, and your insurer may require one.

Keep the photos you took, the plumber's written report and any receipts. They support a claim and show the cause was a sudden failure rather than a long-term leak.

Why pipes burst in Louisville

Freezing

Water expands as it freezes and can split a pipe. The split often shows itself only after the pipe thaws.

Corrosion and age

Older metal pipes thin from the inside and fail at a weak spot.

High water pressure

Pressure above normal strains fittings and fixtures. A pressure check is part of a good repair.

Ground movement

Expansive soil, which the Colorado Geological Survey calls the state's most significant geologic hazard, can stress buried lines.

Thawing a frozen pipe safely

If a pipe is frozen but has not yet burst, open the nearest tap and warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer or a warm towel. Never use an open flame, which is a fire risk and can damage the pipe.

If you find a crack or a bulge, or water starts to flow as the pipe thaws, shut the main and call us. A pipe that froze once will freeze again, so we look at insulation, heat tape and the cold wall that caused it.
