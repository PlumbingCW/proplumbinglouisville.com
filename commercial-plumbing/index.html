Commercial plumbing keeps the doors open

Commercial plumbing covers the drains, water heaters, restrooms, grease lines and supply piping of a business. It differs from home service because downtime costs revenue, and the rules are stricter for food service and public restrooms.

Louisville has an Old Town of shops and restaurants, a Saturday farmers' market on Front Street from May to October and businesses near the US-36 corridor. The 24/7 line is open for after-hours failures.

Restaurant lines often need hydro jetting, and a failed heater needs water heater repair. We serve businesses in Broomfield and Superior as well. Shops and homes around Hecla are covered too.

Business emergency? Protect the space first

Protect customers and stock before you protect the pipe.

  • Close the affected fixture's supply valve or the building's main valve.
  • Stop using the restrooms or kitchen drains if wastewater is rising.
  • Move inventory and equipment out of the water path.
  • Photograph the damage for your insurer and landlord.
  • Call us and tell us whether you own the building or rent, so we can coordinate with the right party.

Planned maintenance prevents the worst calls

Many business emergencies start as small signs. A slow floor drain, a toilet that runs, a heater with a rumble and a drain that gurgles all warn before a failure that closes the doors.

A walk-through before busy season is inexpensive. We note the condition of drains, shutoffs, backflow devices and heaters, and give a written list in the order of urgency.

Backflow, grease and compliance

Food service operators often need grease interceptors maintained, and some systems need backflow preventers tested on a schedule. Ask your health inspector and the water utility which devices apply to your business.

We can service the plumbing side and give you records. Rules differ by business and building, so confirm them with the City before assuming what applies.

Common calls from Louisville businesses

Restaurant drain backups

Grease and food solids clog kitchen lines. Hydro jetting clears them, as described on our hydro jetting page.

Restroom fixture failures

Running toilets, broken flushometers and leaking faucets drive up water bills and annoy customers.

Water heater outages

A commercial heater failure affects dishwashing, cleaning and staff facilities. See water heater repair.

Main line backups

A blocked sewer service lateral can flood a floor drain. The sewer service line is the property owner's responsibility under the City's detail sheets.

Landlords, tenants and who pays

Responsibility for plumbing depends on your lease. Many leases make the tenant responsible for fixtures inside the suite and the landlord responsible for the building's main lines. We write the findings clearly so you can show them to the other party.

Standalone plumbing work in Louisville needs a permit, according to the City's Building Safety division at (303) 335-4584. We handle the permit for installs and larger repairs and coordinate access with the building manager.
