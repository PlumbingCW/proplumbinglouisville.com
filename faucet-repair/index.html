Faucet repairs, from a drip to a spray

Faucet repair fixes worn cartridges, washers, seals and supply lines, or replaces the whole fixture when parts are no longer made. A steady drip can waste a surprising amount of water over a month, and a failed supply line can flood a cabinet.

The 24/7 line is open if water is spraying or will not shut off.

Water under the sink may be a hidden leak, and a stuck valve may point to a bigger installation fix. We handle faucet calls in Broomfield too.

What to do when a faucet will not stop

A fixture that will not shut off needs its own shutoff valve closed.

  • Look under the sink for two small valves, one for hot and one for cold, and turn each clockwise.
  • If there is no valve or it will not turn, close the main water valve.
  • Open the faucet to drain the line and relieve pressure.
  • Put a bowl or towels under any active leak.
  • Call us and tell us the brand if you can read it.

How a faucet works, and why it wears

Most faucets control flow with a cartridge, a ceramic disc or a rubber washer. Each wears from use and from minerals in the water, and each is a replaceable part.

A faucet with a loose handle or a slow drip is usually asking for a cartridge. A faucet that cannot be tightened any further, or with a corroded base, may be past repair and ready for replacement.

Supply lines and shutoff valves

The flexible lines under a sink are a common failure point, and a burst braided line can flood a cabinet in minutes. If yours are old or kinked, replacing them during a faucet job is cheap insurance.

Stuck shutoff valves are another surprise. A valve that has not been turned in years may not close. We replace failing ones so the next repair is easy.

Common faucet problems and the usual cause

Drip from the spout

A worn cartridge, washer or seat. The fix is usually small.

Low pressure at one tap

A clogged aerator or a partly closed supply valve.

Leak at the handle

A worn O-ring or cartridge.

Outdoor spigot dripping inside

A cracked pipe from freezing. Shut the supply and call us.

Outdoor spigots and Colorado winters

A hose left connected over winter holds water in the spigot, which can freeze and split the pipe inside the wall. The split often shows only when the spigot is turned on in spring and water appears in the basement.

Frost-free spigots help, but they need to slope slightly downward to drain. If you see water inside the wall or ceiling after turning on a hose, shut the supply and see burst pipe repair.
