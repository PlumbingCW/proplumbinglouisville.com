Finding a leak before you open a wall

Leak detection locates a water leak without tearing out finished surfaces. The plumber narrows the source with pressure tests, listening gear and thermal imaging, then opens only the spot that matters.

In Louisville, CO, the usual triggers are a high water bill, a warm patch on a slab floor, a musty smell in a finished basement or a meter that keeps turning. The 24/7 line is open.

Once the leak is found, a burst section goes to burst pipe repair and a failed buried line goes to water service line repair. We trace leaks in Lafayette homes as well.

Suspected leak: try this meter test first

You can learn a lot in ten minutes with no tools. It tells us whether water is leaking on the house side of the meter.

  • Close every tap and make sure the dishwasher, washer and ice maker are not running.
  • Find the water meter and look at the small leak indicator or the sweep hand. Write down the reading.
  • Wait fifteen minutes without using water, then read it again.
  • If the dial moved, water is escaping somewhere. Shut the main valve and check whether the dial stops.
  • If the dial still moves with the main closed, the leak is before your valve. Call us, and call the City too, because that portion may be city owned.

Signs of a hidden leak

A bill that jumped

A leak that runs all day uses far more than a dripping tap and shows up on the next bill.

Warm floor spots

A hot water line leaking under a slab can warm the floor above it.

The sound of running water

A faint hiss or trickle at night, when the house is quiet, points to a pressurized line.

Damp drywall or a musty smell

Moisture behind a wall shows up as staining, bubbling paint or a smell that returns.

Where the leak is matters for who owns the repair

The City of Louisville's detail sheets split the water service line into a property-owner portion and a city-owned portion, with a curb stop between them. A leak on the house side of the curb stop is the owner's to repair. A leak on the street side is the City's.

That is why the meter test above matters. If you suspect the leak is on the service line, our water service line repair page explains how that repair works. For everything inside the walls and under the slab, the detection comes first.

Slab leaks and Louisville soils

The Colorado Geological Survey calls expansive soil Colorado's most significant geologic hazard, and notes that swelling ground can disrupt pipelines and sewer lines. Signs of that movement include cracked basement floors and heaved slabs.

We do not claim every slab leak in Louisville comes from soil movement. We do tell owners that a leak under a slab can start from corrosion, a bad joint or shifting ground, and that finding the exact spot is what keeps the repair small.
