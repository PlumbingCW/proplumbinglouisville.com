A mixed neighborhood around Hecla Lake

The City of Louisville's Comprehensive Plan existing conditions appendix says the Hecla neighborhood is named for the area around Hecla Lake and consists of single-family residential, retirement communities and retail. The City's map also shows Hecla Open Space, Hecla Lake and Hecla Drive.

Because homes, retirement communities and retail sit in the same neighborhood, calls from here can range from a failed water heater in a house to a restroom problem at a shop. We serve Hecla as part of our Louisville plumbing service.

If you live in a managed community

Residents of a retirement community or an association-managed building should check who handles plumbing inside a unit before calling anyone. Some communities have maintenance staff or a required contact, and others leave it to the owner. Your lease, ownership documents or the office can tell you which applies.

If you do own the fixture or the water heater, we can help. Know where the shutoff valve for the unit is, because closing it stops most active leaks while you wait. Call and tell us what you see.

Before the plumber arrives

Water that is spreading is the only thing that cannot wait. Close the shutoff valve under the fixture, or the main valve if you cannot find it, and move anything valuable off the floor. A towel along a wet wall buys time.

If the problem is a cold shower, the water heater is usually the cause. Note whether it is gas or electric and whether any light is on, then tell us on the call. That short description helps us decide what to bring. Do not open the tank or the burner compartment yourself.

If the unit is in a shared mechanical room, check whether the management office handles it before you call. When it is yours, tell us the age of the heater if you know it, since that affects whether a repair or a replacement makes sense. A label on the side of the tank usually shows the model.
