A residential neighborhood beside two parks

The City of Louisville's Comprehensive Plan existing conditions appendix says the Lake Park neighborhood contains both single-family and multifamily homes and no commercial uses. It names Lake Park and Cottonwood Park as local parks, and the City's map shows a Lake Park Open Space.

If your home shares a wall

In a townhome or condo, water from a leak can reach a neighbor, and a drain problem can sit in a line you share. Before you call, check your ownership documents or the association to see who maintains what. That tells you whether a problem is yours to fix.

If a leak is active, close the fixture's shutoff valve or your main valve, tell the neighbors who might be affected, and call. Taking a few photos of what you see helps whoever looks at it later.

Before you call

In a condominium or townhome, plumbing can be shared with a neighbor. A drain that backs up in several units points to a shared line, and a leak that comes through a ceiling may start in the unit above. Say so on the call, because the fix may involve your association.

Shut off the fixture valve if water is moving, and stop running the sink or tub if a drain is slow. Take a quick photo of any damage. It helps you describe the problem and is useful if you need to report it to the management office.

Ask the office who is responsible for the pipe that is failing. In many associations the owner covers lines inside the unit and the association covers shared stacks and mains. Knowing the split before the visit avoids a wasted trip and gets the right person on the phone sooner. Keep the office phone number handy too.
