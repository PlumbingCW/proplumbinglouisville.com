Plumbing in Louisville's historic core

The City of Louisville's planning documents describe Old Town as the heart of the city, known for its small-town charm, quaint history and local main street, with both single-family and multifamily homes. This page is for people who live there and call it home.

We serve Old Town as part of our Louisville plumbing service. The rest of this page covers what is known about the area, what to ask when a house is older, and which services people most often start with.

What the City's records say about Old Town

The City's historical building survey says Louis Nawatny platted the town of Louisville in 1878 on a 40-acre site, and that a Kimberly Addition followed in 1911. The survey area covers roughly 68 square blocks, with Lee Avenue, Front Street, County Road and Main Street among its principal north-south streets, and Rex, Parkview, Hutchinson, Elm and Pine Streets among the east-west ones.

That survey looked at 530 properties at the reconnaissance level and focused on buildings more than fifty years old. A plat date tells you when land was divided, not when a particular house or its pipes were installed, so we never guess a home's plumbing from its address.

Questions to ask when the house is older

If you are buying, selling or renovating an older Old Town house, ask what the main drain and the water service are made of, and whether either has been inspected. Nobody can answer that from the street. A camera look at the sewer line is the direct way to find out, and a plumber can explain what the picture shows.

Remember who owns what. According to the City's construction detail sheet, the sewer service line is the property owner's responsibility, and the water service is split between an owner portion and a City portion at the curb stop. Knowing that before a problem starts saves a wasted call.
