Emergency plumbing for Superior homes

Superior is a town of about 13,094 people on the US-36 corridor, and its homes range from the old buildings of Original Town to the subdivisions of Rock Creek Ranch and Sagamore, which were built in the 1990s. Louisville Pro Plumbing is based in neighboring Louisville and takes emergency calls from Superior around the clock.

Call to ask about timing. Whether you live near Original Town or off CO-128A, the number is the same, and so is the plumber's first question: where is the water coming from?

Superior's water, and the Marshall Fire

According to the Town, Superior's water comes from Northern Water's Big Thompson Project by way of Terminal Reservoir, and the Town treats it at its own plant south of Original Town, rated at about 2 million gallons a day. The Town also runs its own wastewater plant, so questions about the main sewer or water quality go to the Town.

The Marshall Fire of December 30, 2021 destroyed 398 structures in Superior, 393 of them residential, according to public reporting. Only seven historic homes in Original Town survived, and Sagamore was reported as totally lost. Many Superior homes are rebuilds, which means new pipe, new code and different questions than an older home raises.

Before We Arrive: what to do in Superior

Know where the water shutoff is, and close it if a pipe is leaking.

Keep a record

Mention whether it is a rebuild, a newer build or older, so we bring the right fittings.

Rebuilds, permits and new plumbing

A rebuilt home needs a full plumbing install and its own inspections. We coordinate with your builder on rough-in, and our plumbing installation page lays out the stages. Superior's building department handles permits in town, so confirm the requirements with them.

If a rebuilt home develops a leak or a heater problem, call the 24/7 line and tell us the house is new. Many problems in new homes come from a loose fitting or a missed connection, which are quick to find and fix.
