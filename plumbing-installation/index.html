Installation is planning plus craft

Plumbing installation adds or relocates fixtures, water lines and drains. It includes sinks, toilets, showers, tubs, laundry hookups, outdoor spigots and the piping that feeds them.

A good installation starts with a plan: where the drain falls, where the vent goes and how the new work ties into the old. Call to ask about timing, and we schedule the work around your contractor.

Heater work in a remodel is covered on water heater installation, and a basement bath often needs a sump pump plan. Rebuilds in Superior follow the same stages.

Projects we install for

Basement bathrooms

A bathroom below the sewer line needs an ejector pit or a sewage pump. Planning it early avoids rework.

Kitchen and bath remodels

A new layout may move supply and drain lines. We coordinate with the cabinet and tile schedule.

Laundry and utility rooms

Washer boxes, drains and a utility sink are small jobs that need correct venting.

Rebuilds after the Marshall Fire

The fire destroyed 553 homes in Louisville. Rebuilds are new construction with full rough-in and finish plumbing.

What to do before the plumbers arrive

Clear access to the work area, and tell us where the main water valve is. Mark the spots where you want fixtures so the rough-in matches the plan.

Do not cut into walls or floors yourself. Do not cover the work area with furniture or stored boxes, and keep children and pets away from open pipe and tools.

Rough-in versus finish plumbing

Rough-in is the plumbing hidden in the walls and floors, such as supply lines, drains and vents. Finish plumbing is what you see, such as faucets, toilets and fixtures.

Inspections happen at both stages. Skipping or delaying the rough-in inspection can mean opening finished walls, so we keep the plumbing schedule in step with the framing and drywall crews.

Choosing fixtures before the walls close

Choose fixtures early. Rough-in dimensions depend on the exact toilet, vanity and shower valve you pick, and a late change can mean moving pipes.

We can give a fixture list for your designer or contractor so everything on the order matches what goes into the wall.

Permits and inspections in Louisville

The City of Louisville lists standalone plumbing work as needing a permit. Building Safety at (303) 335-4584 handles applications, with City Hall at 749 Main Street open Monday through Thursday from 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. and Friday by appointment.

The City adopted the 2024 International Plumbing Code, effective December 6, 2025. Rough-in and final inspections are part of the process, and we schedule them so the work does not stall.

Working with your contractor

On remodels and rebuilds we coordinate with the general contractor on timing. Plumbing has to be in before the walls close and finished before the final inspection, and the order of trades matters.

If the project includes a new water heater, see water heater installation for placement and venting. A basement that also needs water control may need a sump pump.
