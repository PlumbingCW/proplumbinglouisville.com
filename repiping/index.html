When repairs stop being enough

Repiping removes aging supply lines and installs new ones, usually copper or PEX. It is considered when leaks keep reappearing, pressure drops, water comes out discolored or an inspection finds a pipe material with a poor track record.

Call to ask about timing. The work itself is planned, with a written scope, a permit and a schedule that limits the time you are without water.

A single failure may only need leak detection and one repair, and a sudden failure is burst pipe repair. A remodel pairs repiping with plumbing installation. We repipe homes in Broomfield as well.

Signs that point toward repiping

Repeated leaks in different spots

Each repair is cheap, but several in one year say the pipe is failing.

Discolored water

Rusty or brown water that returns after flushing suggests corrosion inside the pipe.

Low pressure throughout

Mineral buildup or corrosion narrows the pipe.

Known problem material

Some pipe materials used in the past have a record of failing early.

Questions to ask any repiping contractor

Ask what material they plan to use and why, how many days the water will be off and who patches the drywall. Ask for a written scope with the permit included and the inspection schedule listed.

A bid that is far below the others may leave out access, patching or the permit. Compare the scopes before you compare the totals.

Sequencing a repipe with other work

If you plan a remodel, repipe first. Opening walls once for plumbing and again for a remodel wastes money. A planned repipe also lets you add shutoff valves in places that make future repairs easy.

If only one run is failing, a partial repipe on that run may be wiser. We explain what each option leaves behind.

Polybutylene and older homes

Polybutylene, a gray plastic pipe, was used in homes from the late 1970s to the mid-1990s and has a record of failing at its fittings. Louisville's population grew from 2,409 in 1970 to 12,361 in 1990, so homes from that growth period may include it, though we cannot say any specific house does.

The only way to know is to look. We check the material where it is visible at the water heater, the main and under sinks. If you see gray plastic with a PB mark, tell us.

Permit, inspection and living through it

The City of Louisville lists standalone plumbing work as needing a permit. Building Safety, at (303) 335-4584, handles it, and the City adopted the 2024 International Plumbing Code effective December 6, 2025. We pull the permit and schedule the inspection.

Most repipes happen in a few days. We open as little drywall as the job requires, and we tell you in advance when the water will be off. A partial repipe may be the smarter move if only one run is failing.
