What a sewer camera inspection shows

A sewer camera inspection runs a small waterproof camera through the sewer service line, usually from the cleanout to the connection with the City main. The crew records the footage, marks the depth of any defect with a locator, and walks you through it afterward.

It is the only way to know the condition of a pipe nobody can see. Without it, a clog is a guess and a repair is a larger guess.

Footage that shows a blockage leads to sewer line cleaning, and footage that shows damage leads to sewer line repair. Buyers in Superior book scopes during inspection periods as well. We also serve Old Town Louisville homes.

Four times a camera visit pays for itself

Most inspections fall into one of these situations.

Before you buy

A home inspector usually does not scope the sewer line. The City's detail sheets assign it to the owner, so a defect becomes yours at closing.

After repeat clogs

A pattern of backups points to something the cable has not removed. The camera names it.

Before a remodel or landscaping

Digging near an unknown line risks hitting it. The locator marks its path and depth.

After a hard winter or a settling house

Soil movement can offset joints. A check catches it before it becomes a backup.

What the camera report should include

A useful inspection ends with more than a verdict. Ask for the footage, a note of where on the line each issue sits and the length of pipe inspected. A defect 40 feet from the house is a different repair from one near the street.

If you are buying a home, bring the report to your agent before the inspection period ends. A clear line is good news, and a defect gives you a basis to ask for a repair or a credit.

What we look for

Roots at joints show as pale hair-like masses entering the pipe. Cracks run along the length or around the circumference. A belly is a low section where water stands after the camera has passed. Offset joints show as a step where one pipe section has slipped from the next.

Different materials fail differently. Older clay and cast iron tend to crack or flake. Plastic pipe more often shows bellies or joint separation. We tell you which you have and what each one implies.

Louisville specifics worth knowing

Louisville's own detail sheets call for the sewer service to be inspected by camera and repaired as needed by the property owner. Some subdivisions also have underdrains that the same sheets assign to the owner.

If your house was rebuilt after the December 2021 Marshall Fire, the line may be new, but a camera still confirms it was installed to grade. In the city, 553 homes were destroyed, and many lots now have new service lines connected to older ones. The camera checks the joint where new meets old.
