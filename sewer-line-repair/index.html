When cleaning stops working, the pipe needs repair

Sewer line repair fixes the pipe itself: a crack, a separated joint, a root-invaded section or a belly that holds water. We locate the defect with a camera, mark its depth and distance from the cleanout, and then explain the repair that fits.

In Louisville, CO, the sewer service line from the house to the connection is the property owner's responsibility. That includes camera inspection and any repair, according to City of Louisville detail sheets, so an informed decision matters before any digging.

A repair decision starts with footage, which is why we recommend a sewer camera inspection first. Where the line only needs clearing, sewer line cleaning costs less. Owners in Lafayette ask the same questions.

What to do when a sewer failure is an emergency

A failed line becomes an emergency when sewage backs into the house or when the yard begins to sink or smell. The 24/7 line treats both as urgent.

  • Stop using water so the house does not push more wastewater into a broken line.
  • Keep children and pets away from soggy ground over the sewer path.
  • Mark any new sinkhole or wet patch and avoid driving over it.
  • Call for an emergency camera visit. A temporary clearing often gets the house usable the same day while the repair is planned.

Repair options and trade-offs

No single method fits every pipe. The camera result decides.

OptionBest whenTrade-off
Spot repairOne crack or joint, pipe otherwise soundFast and targeted, but other weak spots may remain
Trenchless pipe liningLong runs with roots, small cracks and no collapseLittle digging, but a collapsed or badly offset pipe cannot be lined
Pipe burstingOld pipe that has failed over a long lengthReplaces the pipe with minimal trench, needs entry and exit pits
Open-cut replacementCollapsed pipe, a belly, or a line under a place access is easyMost disruptive to yards, driveways and landscaping, but a full fix

Why Front Range soil matters to your sewer line

The Colorado Geological Survey describes swelling clay as Colorado's most significant geologic hazard, and it notes that swelling and shrinking can disrupt pipelines and sewer lines. A line laid in clay can settle into a low spot or pull at joints as the soil moves through wet and dry seasons.

On the camera, that appears as a belly with standing water or a joint that has pulled apart. Cleaning treats the symptom. Repair is what stops the cycle. Our sewer inspection page explains how the camera work is done.
