A sump pump is the last line of defense

A sump pump sits in a pit in the lowest part of a basement or crawlspace. When groundwater fills the pit, a float switch starts the pump and sends water outside through a discharge pipe.

When it fails, the basement is the first to know. The 24/7 line is open. If the water is already rising, start with the steps below.

If the basement keeps taking on water, see basement waterproofing for the larger picture, and rule out a plumbing source with leak detection. We service pumps in Broomfield too.

Pump not working and water rising? Do this

Water and electricity do not mix.

  • Check the breaker and the plug first. A tripped breaker is a common cause.
  • Lift the float with a stick. If the pump starts, the float is stuck or tangled.
  • If the pump runs but does not move water, the discharge line may be frozen or blocked outside.
  • Do not stand in water to touch any electrical panel or outlet.
  • Move belongings to higher ground and call us.

Test your pump before the storm

Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch. The float should rise, the pump should start and the water should drop and stop at the right level. If it hums without moving water, or does not start, call before the next heavy rain.

Clean the pit once a year, check the discharge outlet for ice and debris and make sure the check valve is not clogged. These ten minutes catch many failures.

Where the water should go

The discharge pipe should carry water well away from the foundation and not toward a neighbor. Sending sump water into the sanitary sewer is generally a problem because it overloads the system, so ask the City what is allowed.

In winter, make sure the outlet does not freeze closed. A discharge that ices up can force water back into the pit.

Common reasons a sump pump fails

Stuck float switch

The float jams against the wall of the pit or on debris.

Failed motor

Pumps wear out, and a long run during a storm can finish an old unit.

Frozen or blocked discharge

A line that is clogged with ice or dirt backs water into the pit.

Power loss

A storm can cut power at the same moment the pit fills. A battery backup solves this.

Where the water comes from in Louisville

The Colorado Geological Survey calls expansive soil Colorado's most significant geologic hazard and lists cracked basement floors and heaved slabs as signs of it. Cracks give water a path into a basement, so a pit that fills more often than it used to deserves a closer look.

We do not guess about your lot. We look at the pit, the discharge and the history you tell us. If a pump runs constantly, the answer may be a drainage fix outside, which our basement waterproofing page explains.
