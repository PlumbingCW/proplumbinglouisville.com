What a tankless water heater is, and who it suits

A tankless water heater heats water only when a tap opens, using a gas burner or electric elements and a heat exchanger. It has no tank to run out of, and it takes far less floor space than a 40 or 50 gallon unit.

It suits households with limited space or high hot water demand, and it works best when the installer matches its capacity to the number of fixtures running at the same time. It does not suit every house, and a good installer will say when a tank is the better choice.

Scale or an error code may only need water heater repair, and a first install follows installation planning. We service tankless units in Broomfield homes too. Compact homes in Steel Ranch are part of our service area.

Four things to plan before you buy

Flow rate against temperature rise

A unit's gallons per minute drops as the incoming water gets colder. Winter inlet temperatures on the Front Range are lower than in summer, so size for January, not July.

Gas line capacity

A tankless burner draws more gas at once than a tank. The line to the heater may need upgrading.

Venting

Most gas units need special sealed vent pipe, and elevation of about 5,400 feet means the manufacturer's altitude instructions apply.

Annual service

Scale builds up in the heat exchanger. Regular flushing keeps the unit efficient and the warranty intact.

Is tankless right for your house?

Tankless units pay off most where space is tight or hot water demand is steady. A household that runs two showers and the dishwasher at once needs a larger unit than a couple living alone.

If your gas meter, line size or venting cannot support a tankless burner without costly changes, a high-quality tank may serve better. We would rather tell you that than sell the wrong unit.

Flush and descale: why it matters

Minerals in the water leave scale inside the heat exchanger. As the layer thickens, the unit heats less efficiently and eventually throws an error code or loses flow. A flush circulates a descaling solution through the unit and then rinses it out.

The City of Louisville's water quality report does not list a hardness figure, so test your tap or ask the City for the value. In the meantime, ask us to inspect the inlet filter and the heat exchanger. Many owners are surprised how soon a neglected unit needs help, and a flush is far cheaper than a heat exchanger.

Recirculation and the wait for hot water

A common complaint with tankless units is the delay before hot water reaches a faraway bathroom, and the cold plug of water that can follow a short draw. A recirculating pump and a return line can cut the wait, and some units include one. It adds pump electricity and some wear.

We help you decide whether a recirculation setup fits your house, since a house with a short trunk line may not need it. If your tank water heater is also giving you trouble, our water heater repair page covers diagnosis.
