A planned install gives you choices

Water heater installation covers putting a new unit into a house that does not have one, or moving and upgrading the one it has. Because nothing is leaking, you can compare fuel, efficiency and location, which is not possible during a failure.

In Louisville, CO, installations come from three situations: a remodel that moves the utility room, a new home on a rebuilt lot, and a house that is switching from gas to electric. Planned work is usually scheduled around the rest of your project.

A heater that has already failed is an emergency, so see water heater replacement. Smaller households may prefer a tankless unit. We install in Superior rebuilds as well. We also serve rebuilt and newer homes in Coal Creek Ranch.

Three Louisville situations we install for

Rebuilds after the Marshall Fire

The fire destroyed 553 homes in Louisville, and rebuilds are new construction that must meet the codes in force at permit. We coordinate heater location and venting with the builder.

Remodels and basement finishing

A moved utility room changes the vent path, the gas line and the drain. Planning it early avoids rework.

Gas to electric conversions

Homeowners switching fuel need the right circuit, and a heat pump unit needs space and airflow. We check the panel first.

Additions and accessory dwellings

A second heater or a larger tank may be the better option than stretching the original.

What to do before the crew arrives

Clear a path to the heater and a few feet of working space around it. Move stored items, and tell us about pets and any steps or tight turns on the way.

Know where the main water valve and the gas shutoff are, and keep a bucket and towels handy. Do not try to drain or disconnect the old tank yourself.

Questions to settle before the install day

Decide where the heater will sit, what fuel it will use and who handles the permit. Settling these before the crew arrives avoids a second visit.

Ask what comes with the installation. A drain pan, a new shutoff valve, a thermal expansion tank and a haul-away of the old unit are all line items worth confirming in writing.

Permit and inspection

The City of Louisville lists replacing a water heater as needing a permit, and it lists standalone plumbing work the same way. Building Safety at City Hall, 749 Main Street, can be reached at (303) 335-4584. The City adopted the 2024 International Plumbing Code with an effective date of December 6, 2025, and its Building Safety division handles inspections.

On a new build or a rebuild the heater is often part of a larger permit, and the builder coordinates inspections. We add the heater to that schedule so the final inspection does not stall.

Placement matters more than people expect

A heater needs a drain nearby, room for service access and a vent or airflow that suits the type. It also helps to place it close to the bathrooms and kitchen so hot water travels less pipe. In basements, a pan with a drain line catches small leaks before they spread.

We also look at the shutoff valves and the expansion control in the line, since a pressure-regulated supply needs a place for expanding hot water to go. Our plumbing installation page describes the surrounding work that often comes with a remodel.
