No hot water or a leaking tank: where to start

Water heater repair finds why a tank or tankless unit has stopped heating or started leaking, then fixes the part that failed. In Louisville, CO, a lot of calls come in cold weather, when a heater that was coping suddenly is not.

The 24/7 emergency line answers at any hour. We give a clear price before work starts so you can choose between repair and a new unit.

If the tank is leaking from the steel, the answer is water heater replacement, and a unit that is only scaled may need tankless service instead. We cover Broomfield homes on the same line.

Water heater leaking? Do this first

A leaking tank can drop a lot of water on a finished basement in a short time. These steps limit the damage.

  • Turn the cold water supply valve on top of the heater to off. It is usually a lever or round handle on the inlet pipe.
  • Switch the unit to off. For gas, turn the control to pilot or off. For electric, shut the breaker.
  • If water is reaching outlets or the panel, shut off power to that area only if you can reach the breaker without standing in water.
  • Put a bucket or wet vacuum under a slow drip and move belongings out of the way.
  • Call, and tell us whether the water is from the tank body or the pipes above it.

Common reasons a water heater stops working

The symptom usually points to the failed part.

Gas unit with no heat

A pilot that will not stay lit, a failing thermocouple or a faulty gas control valve are typical causes.

Electric unit with no heat

A tripped breaker, a failed heating element or a bad thermostat.

Lukewarm water that fades fast

A broken dip tube or a failed lower element mixes cold water in. Sediment can also insulate the burner.

Rumbling or popping from the tank

Sediment at the bottom traps water that boils under it. A flush may help, though a tank with heavy buildup may be near the end.

Gas appliances and Louisville's elevation

Louisville sits at about 5,400 feet. Gas appliances come with manufacturer instructions about altitude, and a heater that was installed without adjusting for it can burn poorly, soot, or trip safety devices. If a pilot keeps going out or the flame looks yellow and lazy, tell the plumber the home's elevation and when the heater was installed.

Combustion air and venting also matter in tightly built homes. A carbon monoxide alarm on every level is the right backstop. If it sounds, leave the house and call 911 before you call a plumber.

Water quality and your tank

The City of Louisville's annual water quality report lists chlorine as the disinfectant and reports copper at 0.02 ppm against an action level of 1.3 ppm. It does not list a hardness figure. You can ask the City for it or test your own tap, because minerals in the water drive sediment, and sediment is what shortens a tank's life.

If a tank has not been flushed in years, ask the plumber to look at the drain valve and the anode rod before deciding. Sometimes a flush and a new rod buy years. If the tank is leaking from the steel, the answer is replacement.
